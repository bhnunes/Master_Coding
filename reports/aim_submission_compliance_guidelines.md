# Artificial Intelligence in Medicine Submission Compliance Guidelines

Source reviewed:
`reports/Guide for authors - Artificial Intelligence in Medicine - ISSN 0933-3657 _ ScienceDirect.com by Elsevier.pdf`

Purpose: use this file as a reusable screening guide for `reports/main.tex` and
the final submission package to *Artificial Intelligence in Medicine*. The source
PDF was generated from ScienceDirect on 2026-06-03 and contains 42 pages.

Before final submission, re-check the live journal guide because publisher rules
can change.

## High-Risk Desk-Screening Items

- The manuscript must fit the journal scope: AI in medicine, medically oriented
  human biology, or health care.
- The paper must combine potential high impact in a real medical or health-care
  domain with strong novelty in AI or computer-science method/theory.
- A mere application of known algorithms to medical data is not considered
  sufficiently original for this journal.
- The medical domain must be discussed with proper technical and medical depth.
- Clinical usefulness and potential impact should be assessed or explicitly
  discussed where possible.
- English must be clear and carefully revised; poor English may lead to
  rejection.
- The journal does not publish conference volumes or conference papers. Results
  previously presented at a conference may be submitted only as a thoroughly
  revised, rephrased, and extended original research paper with new research
  results.

## Suitable Article Types

The guide lists these article categories:

- Original research contributions: theoretical or methodological papers about
  novel approaches.
- Methodological reviews or surveys.
- Position papers.
- Editorials and invited guest editorials.
- Letters to the editor.
- Book reviews.
- Errata.
- Historical perspectives.
- In memoriam papers.
- PhD project papers.

For the current manuscript, screen it as an original research contribution unless
the submission strategy changes.

## Editorial Process

- Review is single anonymized.
- Editors first assess suitability for the journal.
- Suitable submissions are typically sent to at least two independent reviewers.
- Final acceptance or rejection is made by the editors.
- Editors do not handle decisions for papers involving their own work, family
  members, colleagues, or products/services in which they have an interest.
- Special issues and article collections follow the same ethical and peer-review
  standards as regular submissions.
- Only one formal appeal per submission is considered, and the appeal decision is
  final.

## Ethics and Publication Policies

### Submission Declaration

By submitting, authors imply that:

- The work has not been published previously except as a preprint, abstract,
  published lecture, academic thesis, or registered report.
- The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.
- Publication is approved by all authors and by the responsible authorities where
  the work was carried out.
- If accepted, the article will not be published elsewhere in the same form, in
  English or another language, including electronically, without permission from
  the copyright holder.
- Elsevier may screen the manuscript for compliance with publishing policies.

### Authorship

- Every author should have made substantial contributions to conception/design,
  data acquisition, or analysis/interpretation.
- Every author should have drafted the article or revised it critically for
  important intellectual content.
- Every author must approve the submitted version.
- Every author is accountable for the accuracy and integrity of the work.
- A single corresponding author must be appointed for communication with the
  journal.
- The corresponding author submits the manuscript and required documentation and
  remains the main contact during review, publication, and post-publication.
- Only the corresponding author's affiliation is used for publishing agreement
  eligibility and possible discounts.

### Changes to Authorship

- Treat the author list and order as final at submission.
- Authorship additions, deletions, rearrangements, or corresponding-author
  changes before acceptance require editor approval.
- Requests must be made by the corresponding author using Elsevier's Authorship
  Change Request form.
- The request must include the reason and written confirmation from all authors,
  including any author being added or removed.
- Authorship changes are not allowed after acceptance.
- Review may pause while a change request is considered.
- Unauthorized authorship changes may lead to rejection or retraction.

### Competing Interests

- All financial and personal relationships that could influence or bias the work
  must be disclosed.
- Examples include employment, consultancies, stock ownership, honoraria, paid
  expert testimony, patents, grants, other funding, and affiliation with the
  journal as an editor or advisory board member.
- The declarations tool must be completed.
- If there are no competing interests, select "I have nothing to declare".
- The generated declaration must be uploaded during submission as a `.doc` or
  `.docx` file; signatures are not required.
- Authors affiliated with the journal must disclose that role and state that they
  had no involvement in peer review or access to peer-review information.

### Funding

- Disclose every funding source that supported the research or manuscript
  preparation.
- Declare the sponsor role in study design, data collection, data analysis,
  interpretation, writing, and the decision to submit.
- If sponsors had no such role, state that explicitly.
- Use a standard funding format with funder names and grant numbers where
  applicable.
- If there was no specific funding, the guide recommends this statement:
  "This research did not receive any specific grant from funding agencies in the
  public, commercial, or not-for-profit sectors."

### Generative AI in Manuscript Preparation

- Authors must declare the use of generative AI or AI-assisted tools in
  manuscript preparation at submission.
- AI tools may support preparation only under human oversight and control.
- AI tools must not replace human critical thinking, expertise, or evaluation.
- Authors remain responsible for accuracy, completeness, impartiality,
  originality, privacy, intellectual-property compliance, and source checking.
- AI tools must not be listed or cited as authors or co-authors.
- If AI tools were used, add a section before the references at first submission:
  `Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript
  preparation process`.
- The statement should name the tool or service, explain the purpose, and state
  that the authors reviewed and edited the content and take full responsibility.
- Basic grammar, spelling, and reference-checking tools do not require
  disclosure.
- Disability-related assistive technologies used solely for accessibility do not
  require disclosure.
- If there is nothing to disclose, no statement is required.

### Preprints

- Preprint sharing is allowed under Elsevier's article sharing policy and does
  not count as prior publication.
- The journal offers optional SSRN posting during submission after initial desk
  review.
- Posting or not posting a preprint has no effect on editorial process or
  publication outcome.
- The corresponding author should seek co-author approval before public SSRN
  posting.

### Inclusive Language

- Use inclusive language throughout.
- Avoid wording that implies superiority or inferiority based on age, gender,
  race, ethnicity, culture, sexual orientation, disability, or health condition.
- Avoid descriptors about personal attributes unless relevant and valid.
- Prefer gender-neutral wording and plural nouns, such as "clinicians" or
  "patients".
- Avoid "he, she" and "he/she" where possible.
- Avoid bias, stereotypes, slang, dominant-culture assumptions, and unsupported
  assumptions about reader beliefs.
- Use either American English or British English consistently; do not mix them.

### Sex- and Gender-Based Analyses

- When research involves or pertains to humans, animals, or eukaryotic cells,
  sex- and gender-based analyses should be integrated into the research design
  where appropriate.
- Address sex and/or gender dimensions in the manuscript, or declare them as a
  limitation to generalizability.
- Explicitly define how sex and/or gender are used to avoid ambiguity or
  conflation.
- Follow funder/sponsor requirements and relevant field best practices.

### Jurisdictional Claims

- Elsevier is neutral regarding territorial disputes and jurisdictional claims.
- Maps should show only the area actually studied.
- Do not include a broader location map beyond the study-area bounding box.
- If maps are used, add a note that map lines delineate study areas and do not
  necessarily depict accepted national boundaries.
- Institutional affiliations should use the full standard institution name or a
  standard abbreviation that can be independently verified.

## Human-Subject, Tissue, and Clinical-Trial Rules

### Ethics Statement

- At submission, authors must confirm whether the research needs an ethics
  statement.
- If required, the statement must confirm compliance with relevant laws and
  institutional guidelines and approval by the appropriate institutional
  committee.
- The statement should include the date and reference number of ethical approvals.
- The statement should confirm informed consent for experimentation with human
  subjects.
- Privacy rights of human subjects must always be observed.
- If no ethics statement is required, explain why.

### Studies Involving Humans, Human Data, or Biological Material

- Follow relevant laws, regulations, institutional guidelines, the Declaration of
  Helsinki where applicable, and ICMJE recommendations.
- Research should aim to be representative of human populations in terms of sex,
  age, and ethnicity where relevant.
- Informed consent must be obtained for research involving human participants,
  their data, or biological materials.
- Consent must be free from coercion.
- Consent for publication should be obtained when applicable.
- Privacy rights must always be observed.
- Identifiable participant materials require explicit written consent.
- Participant information must be de-identified; do not include names, initials,
  ID numbers, dates of birth, or similar identifiers.
- Images should be used only when scientifically necessary and with explicit
  consent.
- Nonessential identifying details must be removed.
- If identifiers are altered for anonymity, confirm that scientific meaning is not
  affected.
- Fully anonymized images without identifying marks and without identifying text
  do not require formal consent, though informing the patient is good practice.
- Case reports always require consent.
- Obscuring identity by eye bars or blurring is not sufficient without consent.
- Written consents must be retained by the authors and provided to the journal
  only if specifically requested.
- Research with vulnerable populations may require additional safeguards and
  ethical approval.
- If ethics approval is not required or an exemption was granted, the manuscript
  must explain why and confirm that participant rights and privacy were protected.

### Human Organs or Tissues

- For studies using human organs or tissues, provide sufficient evidence that
  material was procured in line with WHO Guiding Principles on Human Cell,
  Tissue and Organ Transplantation.
- The source of organs or tissues must be transparent and traceable.
- Organs or tissues must not be sourced from executed prisoners or prisoners of
  conscience.
- For clinical studies, informed consent plus suitable ethical approval may be
  sufficient, but the journal may request additional evidence.
- If the manuscript describes organ transplantation, declare that autonomous
  consent, free from coercion, was obtained from donors or next of kin, and that
  organs/tissues were not sourced from executed prisoners or prisoners of
  conscience.

### Clinical Trials

- The journal follows ICMJE clinical-trial registration guidelines.
- Clinical trials must be registered in a public trial registry at or before the
  onset of patient enrollment.
- Purely observational studies where intervention assignment is not controlled by
  the investigator do not require registration.
- The clinical trial registration number must be included in the manuscript,
  preferably at the end of the abstract.
- Clinical trials include prospective assignment of human participants or groups
  to health-related interventions to evaluate health outcomes.
- Authors must disclose registry postings of results for the same or closely
  related work.
- Brief structured registry results under 500 words or in a table are not treated
  as prior publication when posted in the primary registry.
- Other result disclosures, such as investor meetings, are discouraged and may
  jeopardize consideration.
- Randomized controlled trials should follow CONSORT and provide the CONSORT
  checklist plus a flow diagram.

## Manuscript File and Source Requirements

- Provide editable source files for the full submission, including text, tables,
  figures, and text graphics.
- Save LaTeX source with `.tex`; a PDF alone is not an acceptable source file.
- Double-column formatting is permitted for LaTeX submissions.
- Use the Elsevier LaTeX template when preparing a LaTeX submission.
- Provide all relevant editable LaTeX source files at submission or revision.
- Remove strikethrough and underlined text unless scientifically meaningful.
- Run spelling and grammar checks before submission.

For this repository, the local build already uses the Elsevier `cas-dc` LaTeX
class through `reports/main.tex`. The final package should still include every
required editable source file, bibliography file, and figure file.

## Title Page and Front Matter

### Title

- The article title should be concise and informative.
- Avoid abbreviations and formulae unless established and widely understood.

### Authors and Affiliations

- Provide given names and family names for every author.
- Author order in the manuscript must match the order in the submission system.
- Check the spelling of all author names.
- Affiliations should reflect where the work was carried out.
- Include full affiliation addresses, country names, and, if available, author
  email addresses.
- Clearly identify the corresponding author.
- Keep the corresponding author's email and contact details current throughout
  review, publication, and post-publication.
- Use present/permanent address footnotes only when an author moved or was
  visiting during the work.

### Abstract

- Maximum length: 250 words.
- The abstract must be concise and factual.
- Briefly state purpose, principal results, and major conclusions.
- The abstract must stand alone.
- Avoid references; if essential, give author names and years.
- Avoid nonstandard or uncommon abbreviations; if essential, define them at first
  mention inside the abstract.

### Keywords

- Provide 1 to 7 keywords.
- Keywords must be in English.
- Avoid keyword phrases with "and" or "of" where possible.
- Use abbreviations only if firmly established in the field.

### Highlights

- Highlights are encouraged at submission.
- Submit highlights as a separate editable file in the online submission system.
- Include the word "highlights" in the file name.
- Provide 3 to 5 bullet points.
- Each bullet must be at most 85 characters, including spaces.
- Highlights should capture novel results and any new methods.

## Manuscript Body and Structure

- Divide the manuscript into clearly defined, numbered sections.
- Number subsections as `1.1`, `1.1.1`, `1.1.2`, `1.2`, and so on.
- Use section numbering when cross-referencing; do not refer only to "the text".
- Give subsections brief headings on separate lines.
- Do not include the abstract in section numbering.
- If using a theory section, it should extend the introduction and lay the
  foundation for further work.
- If using a calculation section, it should present practical development from
  the theoretical basis.
- Place acknowledgements in a separate section directly before the references.
- Do not place acknowledgements on the title page, as a title footnote, or
  elsewhere in the manuscript.

## Author Contributions

- Corresponding authors are required to acknowledge co-author contributions using
  CRediT roles.
- Use only roles that apply, and allow multiple roles per author.
- Available CRediT roles listed by the guide:
  Conceptualization, Data curation, Formal analysis, Funding acquisition,
  Investigation, Methodology, Project administration, Resources, Software,
  Supervision, Validation, Visualization, Writing - original draft, and
  Writing - review and editing.

## Units, Math, Tables, and Appendices

### Units

- Use the International System of Units (SI).
- If other units are mentioned, provide the equivalent SI unit.

### Math Formulae

- Submit equations as editable text, not images.
- Present simple formulae inline where possible.
- Use `/` for small fractional terms such as `X/Y`.
- Present variables in italics.
- Use `exp` for powers of `e`.
- Display equations separately from text when needed.
- Number displayed equations consecutively in the order they are referred to.

### Tables

- Submit tables as editable text, not images.
- Place tables next to relevant text or on separate pages at the end.
- Cite every table in the manuscript text.
- Number tables consecutively according to first appearance.
- Provide captions for all tables.
- Put table notes below the table body.
- Avoid vertical rules and cell shading.
- Use tables sparingly and avoid duplicating results already described elsewhere.

### Appendices

- Identify appendices as `A`, `B`, and so on.
- Number appendix equations separately, e.g. `Eq. (A.1)`, `Eq. (B.1)`.
- Number appendix tables and figures separately, e.g. `Table A.1`, `Fig. A.1`.

## Figures, Images, Artwork, and Video

### Artwork Files

- Supply figures, images, diagrams, and other artwork as separate files with the
  manuscript.
- Cite every image in the manuscript text.
- Number images in the order they appear.
- Use logical file names such as `Figure_1`, `Figure_2`.
- Text graphics may be embedded in the text; LaTeX text graphics may also be
  embedded in the LaTeX file.
- Every artwork item needs a caption.
- Captions should include a brief title, not displayed on the figure itself, and
  a description.
- Keep figure text to a minimum.
- Explain every symbol or abbreviation used in the figure.

### Artwork Formats and Resolution

- Vector drawings: EPS or PDF, with fonts embedded or text saved as graphics.
- Color or grayscale photographs: TIFF, JPG, or PNG at minimum 300 dpi
  (minimum 1063 pixels for single-column width or 2244 pixels for full-page
  width).
- Bitmapped line drawings: TIFF, JPG, or PNG at minimum 1000 dpi
  (minimum 3543 pixels for single-column width or 7480 pixels for full-page
  width).
- Combined line/halftone artwork: TIFF, JPG, or PNG at minimum 500 dpi
  (minimum 1772 pixels for single-column width or 3740 pixels for full-page
  width).
- Do not submit low-resolution artwork.
- Do not submit artwork where text is too small relative to the image.
- Do not combine different images or graphs into one file, because this harms
  accessibility.

### Color and Accessibility

- Usable color figures will appear in color online.
- Make color images accessible to readers with impaired color vision.
- Print color reproduction may involve costs after acceptance.

### Generative AI in Artwork

- AI tools may support explanatory images such as flowcharts, conceptual
  diagrams, or schematic illustrations.
- AI tools may create data visualizations only when the output is directly
  derived from underlying data using reproducible analytical, computational, or
  statistical methods.
- AI tools must not create or alter primary observed or experimental data images,
  including microscopy, histology, patient images, western blots, and similar
  materials.
- Prohibited primary-data alterations include AI-based brightness, contrast, or
  color-balance changes.
- Primary-data image adjustments should be done only with established image
  processing software.
- If generative AI was used for manuscript images, disclose this both in each
  relevant caption and in the general generative-AI disclosure statement.
- Authors are responsible for image accuracy and originality.

### Supplementary Material

- Supplementary material should be accurate and relevant.
- Cite every supplementary file in the manuscript text.
- Submit supplementary material at the same time as the manuscript.
- Provide a concise descriptive caption for each supplementary file.
- After submission, supplementary files can only be added or replaced at revision.
- Supplementary materials appear online exactly as submitted and are not checked,
  formatted, or typeset by production.

### Video and Animation

- The journal accepts video material and animation sequences.
- When linking video or animation in the article, add a note in the manuscript
  where the file should be placed.
- Label files clearly and use names directly related to content.
- Use recommended file formats.
- Preferred maximum size: 150 MB per file and 1 GB total.
- Provide a still image for each video or animation file.
- Provide descriptive manuscript text for accessibility and print readers.

## Research Data, Code, and Related Outputs

- The journal encourages transparent storage, access, and discovery of research
  data.
- Research data includes observations or experiments validating findings, plus
  software, code, models, algorithms, protocols, methods, and other useful
  project materials.
- The journal uses Elsevier research-data policy Option B: authors are encouraged
  to deposit research data in a relevant repository, cite/link the dataset in the
  article, or explain why sharing is not possible.
- Data availability should be stated at submission where possible.
- Data statements may be required by a funder or institution.
- If data cannot be accessed or posted, state the reason, such as sensitive or
  confidential patient data.
- A data statement will appear with the published article.
- Provide repository links during online submission when data are available.
- Use persistent identifiers and in-text database identifiers where relevant,
  e.g. `Database: 12345`.
- Consider co-submitting related data, methods, or protocols as a separate Data
  in Brief or MethodsX manuscript if useful for reproducibility.

## References

### Citation Consistency

- Every in-text citation must appear in the reference list.
- Every reference-list entry must be cited in the text.
- References cited in the abstract must be given in full.
- Unpublished results and personal communications should generally not appear in
  the reference list.
- If unpublished results or personal communications are included in the reference
  list, format them in the journal style and replace the publication date with
  "unpublished results" or "personal communication".
- "In press" means the item has been accepted for publication.
- Check all reference data before submission, including author surnames, titles,
  years, volumes, article numbers, pages, and DOIs.
- DOIs are encouraged for source linking.

### Reference Style

- At submission, references may use any consistent style.
- Where applicable, include author names, journal or book titles, chapter or
  article titles, publication year, volume numbers, article numbers, and pages.
- The journal style is applied after acceptance, but the guide specifies numeric
  in-text citations in square brackets.
- Reference-list order should follow first appearance in the text.
- Use shortened final page numbers, e.g. `51-9`.
- For more than six authors, list the first six followed by `et al.`.
- Abbreviate journal names according to the List of Title Word Abbreviations.

### Web, Dataset, Software, and Preprint References

- Web references need at minimum the full URL and access date.
- Add DOI, author names, dates, or source publication information where known.
- Web references may appear in the main reference list or under a separate
  heading after the reference list.
- Dataset references should include author names, dataset title, repository,
  version where available, year, and global persistent identifier.
- Add `[dataset]` before dataset references so Elsevier can identify them; this
  tag will not appear in the published article.
- Software references should include software name, version where available,
  repository or archive, date, and DOI/persistent identifier where available.
- Preprints must be clearly marked with "preprint" or the preprint server name
  and should include the preprint DOI.
- If a preprint has become a peer-reviewed publication, cite the formal
  publication instead.
- Remove reference-manager field codes before submission.

## Submission Package Checklist

- One corresponding author is designated.
- Corresponding-author email address, full postal address, and phone numbers are
  provided.
- Manuscript source files are uploaded and editable.
- All figures/artwork are uploaded as separate files where required.
- Tables, table footnotes, and captions are included.
- Video files and supplementary materials are uploaded if used.
- Highlights are uploaded as a separate editable file if provided.
- Spelling and grammar checks are complete.
- All in-text citations and reference-list entries match.
- Permissions are obtained for any copyrighted material from other sources,
  including web material.
- Any required ethics statement, consent statement, clinical-trial registration,
  CRediT statement, funding statement, competing-interest declaration, data
  statement, and generative-AI statement are present.
- All authors understand open-access APC responsibility if choosing open access.
- The submission is made through the journal's online submission system.
- The online system will convert source files into a single PDF for peer review,
  but editable files remain required for final typesetting.

## After Acceptance or Final Decision

- Elsevier may recommend transfer to another Elsevier journal through the Article
  Transfer Service.
- After acceptance, the corresponding author completes the publishing agreement.
- Authors choose license options, considering funder requirements.
- Open-access publication may involve an APC.
- Written permission is required for copyrighted material from other sources, and
  the source must be credited in the article.
- Proof corrections are normally due within two days.
- Proofs are for checking typesetting, editing, completeness, and correctness of
  article text, tables, and figures.
- Significant changes at proof stage require journal-editor approval.
- A 50-day Share Link is provided for non-open-access articles.
- Open-access articles are shared through the DOI because the final version is
  openly available on ScienceDirect.
- Share and promote the article according to Elsevier responsible-sharing rules.

## Future Screening Workflow for `reports/main.tex`

Use this checklist before submission:

- [ ] Confirm the manuscript explicitly states the AI/computer-science novelty,
      not only the medical application.
- [ ] Confirm the real-world medical domain and clinical relevance are discussed
      with sufficient depth.
- [ ] Confirm the manuscript does not overclaim clinical deployment or external
      generality beyond the evidence.
- [ ] Confirm title, authors, affiliations, and corresponding-author data are
      complete and consistent with the submission system.
- [ ] Count abstract words and keep the abstract at or below 250 words.
- [ ] Confirm the abstract is stand-alone and avoids undefined abbreviations.
- [ ] Confirm there are 1 to 7 English keywords.
- [ ] Confirm highlights contain 3 to 5 bullets and every bullet is at most
      85 characters including spaces.
- [ ] Prepare highlights as a separate editable file for upload.
- [ ] Confirm all sections/subsections are numbered appropriately by the LaTeX
      template and cross-references are precise.
- [ ] Add or verify acknowledgement, CRediT, funding, competing-interest, data
      availability, ethics/consent, and generative-AI sections as applicable.
- [ ] Verify whether the DIAGSET work requires an ethics statement or an
      exemption explanation because it uses human-derived histopathology data.
- [ ] Confirm all figures are cited, captioned, legible, color-accessible, and
      available as separate high-resolution files.
- [ ] Confirm no AI-generated or AI-altered primary histology/patient imagery is
      used.
- [ ] Confirm all tables are editable, cited, captioned, consecutively numbered,
      and free of vertical rules/shading where possible.
- [ ] Confirm equations are editable text and numbered in order if displayed.
- [ ] Confirm all units are SI or have SI equivalents.
- [ ] Confirm every citation appears in the bibliography and every bibliography
      entry is cited.
- [ ] Confirm web references have access dates.
- [ ] Confirm dataset, software, code, and preprint references are clearly
      labeled and include persistent identifiers where available.
- [ ] Confirm all source files, bibliography files, figures, supplementary files,
      captions, and permission files are ready for upload.
- [ ] Build `reports/main.pdf` from the editable sources and inspect the log for
      missing references, missing figures, serious overfull boxes, and other
      production risks.
